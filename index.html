An assistive technology project

A walking stick that cues people through freezing of gait

Freezing of gait stops the feet mid-step while the brain is still sending the signal to walk. External cueing, a rhythm you can hear, feel or see, is an established clinical technique for restarting movement. This stick builds all three into the handle.

A working research prototype. Not available to buy, and not a medical device.

Why it exists

It started with my grandad

My grandad lives with Parkinson's. I watched freezing of gait take away his confidence to cross a room: the unpredictability of it, and the independence it quietly removes.

I'm an engineer. Freezing of gait is a problem with an established clinical response and no affordable device that delivers it in something people already carry every day. That gap is why this exists.

The device

Three cues, one familiar object

Research into external cueing reports that combining cues works better than any one alone. The stick delivers all three, in something people already carry.

Rhythm you hear

A steady beat from a built-in speaker, set to match the user's own walking pace.

Rhythm you feel

The handle pulses in time with the beat, so the cue works in noisy places.

A line you step over

A laser line projected on the floor gives a visible target.

Where it's got to

People with Parkinson's have used it

In April 2026 I took the prototype to the Parkinson's UK Southend support group. People held it, felt the vibration, heard the beat, and told me it was something they would want to use. That led to conversations with Parkinson's UK Tech Partnerships and with PD Buddy.

The project now continues as a Master of Research at Swansea University, where the next steps are a second prototype and structured gait testing.
